Best Budgeting Apps: YNAB, Monarch, or Simple Excel Spreadsheets?

NeonDrifter
25.08.2026 • Read time: 4 min

Managing personal finances is one of the most critical steps toward achieving long-term financial freedom, yet finding the right tool for the job can feel overwhelming. Whether you are trying to pay off debt, build an emergency fund, or optimize your savings, the debate usually boils down to three distinct contenders: You Need A Budget (YNAB), Monarch Money, and traditional Excel spreadsheets. Each option offers a radically different philosophy on money management, catering to different personalities, tech-savviness levels, and financial goals.

As you navigate your financial journey—perhaps even supplementing your income by learning how to start freelancing with zero experience: a step-by-step guide—having a firm grip on your cash flow becomes even more essential. Selecting the right budgeting method is the first step toward turning financial anxiety into absolute clarity. Let’s dive deep into a comprehensive comparison of YNAB, Monarch, and Excel to help you decide which tool deserves a spot in your digital ecosystem.

Understanding the Contenders: An Overview

Before pitting them against one another, it helps to understand what each platform stands for and who it was designed to serve. The personal finance landscape has evolved dramatically over the last decade, moving away from manual ledger books toward automated cloud syncing and highly customized digital templates.

You Need A Budget (YNAB): The Philosophy of Zero-Based Budgeting

YNAB is not just an app; it is a methodology based on four strict rules designed to change your relationship with money. YNAB utilizes a zero-based budgeting system, meaning every single dollar you earn is assigned a specific job before you spend it. If you earn $4,000 this month, every dollar must be allocated toward rent, groceries, savings, or debt until your "To Be Budgeted" amount hits zero.

YNAB forces you to be proactive rather than reactive. Instead of looking backward at what you spent last month, you plan forward for what your money needs to do right now. While it has a steeper learning curve than automated trackers, its dedicated followers swear by its ability to break the paycheck-to-paycheck cycle.

Monarch Money: The Modern, Automated All-In-One Dashboard

If YNAB is about strict behavioral change, Monarch Money is about seamless visibility and modern aesthetics. Launched as a direct competitor to Mint (following its shutdown), Monarch is a beautifully designed, highly customizable dashboard that connects to thousands of financial institutions. It aggregates your checking accounts, credit cards, investments, loans, and crypto holdings into a single, cohesive view.

Monarch offers robust tracking features, collaborative tools for couples, goal-setting widgets, and detailed cash-flow reports. It doesn't force you into a specific budgeting philosophy; instead, it adapts to how you naturally spend, making it exceptionally user-friendly for beginners and seasoned budgeters alike.

Simple Excel Spreadsheets: Total Control and Ultimate Customization

Long before apps could automatically sync with your bank accounts, personal finance lived entirely in spreadsheets. Using Excel (or Google Sheets) remains a powerhouse method for managing money. With a spreadsheet, you build the rules, formulas, and categories from scratch, or you download a pre-made template tailored to your precise needs.

While spreadsheets lack automated bank syncing (unless you use third-party plugins or manually import CSV files), they offer unmatched privacy, zero subscription fees, and infinite flexibility. Whether you want to track your monthly spending or meticulously calculate the yield on your cash reserves—perhaps comparing options similar to researching high-yield savings accounts vs. CDs: where should you keep your emergency fund?—Excel lets you structure your data exactly how you want it.

Cost and Value

When looking at price tags, Excel clearly wins by being entirely free (assuming you already have access to Microsoft Office or Google Sheets). Both YNAB and Monarch operate on a subscription model, typically hovering around $99 per year after a free trial.

While paying for a budgeting app might seem counterintuitive, many users find that the subscription cost pays for itself tenfold by curbing unnecessary impulse spending. However, if you are strictly trying to cut unnecessary software expenses from your life, Excel is the obvious choice.

Automation and Bank Syncing

Automation is the holy grail for busy professionals. Both YNAB and Monarch connect directly to financial institutions to pull in transactions automatically. Monarch stands out here by partnering with multiple data aggregators (like Plaid, Finicity, and MX), which reduces the frequency of broken bank connections.

Excel, on the other hand, requires manual data entry or tedious CSV downloads. While some find manual entry a chore, many financial experts argue that typing out every single purchase builds a psychological awareness of spending that automated apps simply cannot replicate.

Deep Dive: Pros and Cons of Each Option

YNAB: Pros & Cons

  • Pros: Exceptionally effective at changing spending habits; excellent educational resources and customer support; forces you to live on last month's income over time.
  • Cons: Expensive annual fee; strict methodology that doesn't appeal to casual trackers; steep learning curve can frustrate newcomers.

Monarch Money: Pros & Cons

  • Pros: Gorgeous, clean user interface; multi-institution aggregation; amazing collaborative features for spouses; comprehensive net worth and investment tracking.
  • Cons: Less aggressive about changing spending behavior compared to YNAB; subscription fee required; relies on third-party bank connections that occasionally drop.

Excel Spreadsheets: Pros & Cons

  • Pros: 100% free; total privacy and offline capability; infinite customization; no risk of third-party security breaches of bank credentials.
  • Cons: Requires manual data input; no automatic alerts or real-time bank syncing out of the box; formulas can break if accidentally edited.

Which One Should You Choose?

The "best" budgeting tool is entirely dependent on your financial psychology, tech habits, and personal preferences. There is no one-size-fits-all answer, but you can narrow down your choice by asking yourself a few simple questions.

Choose YNAB If:

You struggle with overspending, live paycheck-to-paycheck despite making a decent income, and want a rigorous system that forces you to plan every dollar. If you are willing to invest time into learning their zero-based system, YNAB can completely overhaul your financial health.

Choose Monarch Money If:

You want a gorgeous, all-in-one dashboard to track your net worth, investments, and monthly cash flow without rigid rules. It is arguably the best choice for couples who want to manage their finances together transparently with minimal friction.

Choose Excel Spreadsheets If:

You love data, value absolute privacy, want to avoid recurring monthly software subscriptions, and don't mind spending a little extra time manually logging receipts or downloading bank statements.

Conclusion

Ultimately, the best budgeting tool is the one you will actually use consistently. A sophisticated app like YNAB or Monarch is useless if you ignore it for three months, just as an advanced Excel sheet serves no purpose if you never open it. Assess your goals, test out the free trials, and pick the system that empowers you to take control of your financial future with confidence.
